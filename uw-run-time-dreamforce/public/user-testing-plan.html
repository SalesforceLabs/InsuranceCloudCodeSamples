Usability Test Plan

Agentic Insurance Underwriting Workbench

A moderated usability study to evaluate how commercial underwriters intake a new submission, review AI-extracted data, and resolve discrepancies and duplicate submission lines within the agentic workbench prototype.

Audience: Commercial P&C Underwriters Method: Moderated, task-based (think-aloud) Session length: 45–60 min Format: 1:1, in-person or remote screen-share Prototype: NexGen Biologics hero submission

1 · Study Objectives

What we want to learn from putting this prototype in front of underwriters.

Primary goals

  • Determine whether underwriters can move a new submission through the intake-to-submission-lines flow with minimal guidance.
  • Assess trust and comprehension of agent-driven automation (partial extraction, classification, reconciliation) — do underwriters understand what the agent did, and do they trust it?
  • Evaluate the clarity of the discrepancy & duplicate resolution model: reconciling attributes, selecting canonical sources, mapping to canonical terms, and merging duplicate submission lines.
  • Measure whether the Needs Attention queue and Pending Tasks communicate what requires human action versus what the agent handled.

Key questions

  • Where do underwriters expect to start, and does the landing/list view match that expectation?
  • Is the difference between Submission Attributes, Submission Parties, and Lines of Business clear?
  • Can they locate the source of an extracted value and confidently choose the correct one when sources conflict?
  • Does the confidence signalling (low-confidence badges, warning triangles) drive the right level of scrutiny?

3 · Setup & Logistics

Environment

  • Chrome (latest), desktop, ≥1440px width.
  • Prototype reset to Step 1 before each session (clear localStorage / use a fresh browser profile).
  • Hero record: NexGen Biologics Inc — New Business (top of the list, red NEW badge).
  • Setup admin app is out of scope — do not navigate to the gear icon unless probing configuration expectations.

Roles & kit

  • Moderator — runs the script, keeps the participant thinking aloud.
  • Notetaker — logs task success, errors, quotes, time.
  • Screen + audio recording (with consent).
  • Consent form, incentive, this plan, and a printed observation grid.
Demo-flow reminder: The prototype advances through 9 scripted steps via the bottom-right step controller. For most tasks the moderator drives the step; participants act within the current step. Note in the script where the moderator advances the flow.

4 · Moderation Guide

Intro script (read aloud)

“Thanks for joining. Today you'll try an early prototype of an underwriting workbench that uses AI agents to help intake and prepare a new submission. We're testing the software, not you — there are no wrong answers. Please think out loud: tell me what you're looking at, what you expect to happen, and anything that surprises or frustrates you. I may not answer questions right away so I can see how you'd naturally work through it.”

Facilitation rules

  • Ask “What do you expect to happen if you click that?” before they click.
  • When stuck, count to ~15 seconds of silence before offering a nudge; note that a nudge was needed.
  • Probe trust moments: “The agent filled this in — how confident are you in it? What would you do next?”
  • Never demonstrate the path; redirect with “Where would you look?”
Rate each task: 1 Completed unaided · 2 Completed with minor nudge · 3 Completed with significant help · 4 Failed / abandoned. Capture time-on-task and error count per task.

5 · Scenario Context

Give the participant this framing before Task 1.

“You're an underwriter on the commercial team. A broker, Niki Paoloni at Vanguard Insurance Partners, has emailed a new-business submission for NexGen Biologics Inc, a pharmaceutical manufacturer. The submission covers Commercial Property and General Liability. Your job today is to work it from the moment it lands in your queue through to reviewing the assembled submission lines — leaning on the AI agent where it helps, and stepping in where your judgment is needed.”

6 · Task Scenarios

Ordered to follow the natural intake flow. Each maps to real prototype features and a scripted demo step.

1
Find and open the new submission
Step 1List view
“Start on your submissions home. A new submission just arrived — open it.”
What we observe
  • Do they notice the NEW badge / notification bell?
  • Do they use the Run my day vs All submissions tabs meaningfully?
  • Row click vs. notification — which entry point feels natural?
Success criteria
  • Opens the NexGen Biologics record page.
Post-task probe
  • “How would you normally decide which submission to work first?”
2
Understand what the agent did on arrival
Steps 1–2Agentic trust
“The submission just landed. Without doing anything yet — tell me what has happened so far and what, if anything, the system is doing on its own.”
What we observe
  • Can they narrate the agent's partial extraction from the activity feed / pending tasks?
  • Do they distinguish agentic tasks from manual ones?
  • Reaction to the email-received → extraction progression (moderator advances to Step 2).
Success criteria
  • Articulates that the agent read the email and began extracting data.
Post-task probe
  • “How much of this would you want automated vs. done by you?”
3
Classify & extract the attached documents
Step 3Documents
“Some documents came in with the submission. Get them classified and extracted.”
What we observe
  • Locate the Documents tab and the Classify and Extract action.
  • Reaction to auto-classification of ACORD forms vs. the amber “Unable to classify — manually select” file.
  • Comfort correcting / confirming a document category & type.
Success criteria
  • Completes classification for all docs (manual pick for the unclassified one) and saves.
Post-task probe
  • “Did you trust the auto-classification? What made the unclassified file different?”
4
Triage the Needs Attention queue
Steps 4–8Needs Attention
“As the submission progresses, things surface for you. Walk me through what needs your attention and clear anything you can.”
What we observe
  • Do they read the Needs Attention count and tiles as actionable?
  • Use of a tile's Mark as Complete vs. clicking through to the relevant tab.
  • Understanding of dependent tasks (connectors between task cards).
  • Handling of manual tasks with a Run action (e.g. qualifying / data-completion checks).
Success criteria
  • Clears at least one attention item and explains what each remaining item is asking for.
Post-task probe
  • “Is it clear which items are yours to act on vs. the agent's?”
5
Review reconciled Submission Attributes
Step 9Submission Data
“Reconciliation is complete. Open the submission's data and check the account-level attributes look right.”
What we observe
  • Navigate to Submission Data → Submission Attributes.
  • Do the three sub-tabs (Attributes / Parties / Lines of business) read as distinct?
  • Use of search, Source filter, Show unmapped only, expand/collapse.
  • Notice the low-confidence badges and the Extracted Data tab's Status column.
Success criteria
  • Locates a mapped attribute and states its value + which document it came from.
Post-task probe
  • “What does 'canonical term' mean to you here? Is that language you'd use?”
6
Resolve an attribute-mapping discrepancy
Step 9Resolve Attribute Mapping
“The system flagged some attributes where the sources disagree. Resolve them and pick the value you'd trust.”
What we observe
  • Find and act on the Resolve Attribute Mapping card (count badge, Resolve link).
  • Use of the Select Source modal: reading the doc canvas, switching source tabs, seeing the highlighted value.
  • Choosing a preferred source (radio), entering a manual value, or adding an override.
  • Whether the highlighted value in the document builds trust in the choice.
Success criteria
  • Selects a source (or manual value) for a conflicting attribute; the card count decrements.
Post-task probe
  • “How did you decide which source wins? What would you need to feel fully confident?”
7
Explore a Line of Business & its submission lines
Step 9Submission Lines
“Open the Commercial Property line and get familiar with how its locations, buildings and coverages are laid out.”
What we observe
  • Navigate from an LOB tile / Lines of business tab to the submission-line tree.
  • Comprehension of the tree hierarchy (Location → Building → Equipment/Contents → Coverage).
  • Selecting a line to open the sliding detail panel; reading the AI Summary.
  • Interpretation of the warning-triangle markers (discrepancy / potential duplicate).
Success criteria
  • Opens a Location's detail panel and describes its attributes/summary in their own words.
Post-task probe
  • “Does this structure match how you think about a property schedule?”
8
Reconcile a line's attributes via View Sources
Step 9View Sources / Reconcile
“The Chicago Warehouse location has attributes pulled from multiple documents. Review the sources and make sure each attribute is mapped correctly.”
What we observe
  • Open View Sources; understand Mapped vs Unmapped vs Discrepancies tabs.
  • Picking the preferred extraction (radio per term); returning an item to the pool.
  • Mapping an unmapped extraction to a canonical term (single + bulk).
  • Use of the flag rail / document highlighting to verify a value.
  • Marking a discrepancy resolved.
Success criteria
  • Resolves at least one flagged term and maps one unmapped attribute.
Post-task probe
  • “Was it clear what 'unmapped' meant and why you'd map something?”
9
Resolve duplicate submission lines
Step 9Resolve Duplicates
“The agent thinks a couple of these records might be duplicates. Review them and decide whether to merge.”
What we observe
  • Locate the Resolve Duplicate Submission Lines card / flow.
  • Reading the side-by-side comparison + AI Summary row; confidence in the merge decision.
  • Use of Merge Selected / Do Not Merge / Skip.
  • (Optional) Manual merge from the tree toolbar (select 2+ same-type lines → Merge).
Success criteria
  • Makes a merge decision on at least one duplicate group and understands the outcome.
Post-task probe
  • “What information would you need to safely merge two records?”
10
Restructure the schedule (Move / Create / Edit)
Step 9Line CRUD
“A building was filed under the wrong location, and there's a coverage missing. Fix the structure.”
What we observe
  • Use of Move (reparenting with type constraints) — do the allowed-target rules make sense?
  • Adding a line via Add / Add Child and the New Submission Line modal (parent lookup).
  • Editing a line; understanding of neutral Delete and cascade to children.
  • Multi-select behavior (checkboxes, mass move/delete).
Success criteria
  • Successfully moves a line under a valid new parent and creates one new child line.
Post-task probe
  • “Were the rules about where things can move clear? Did anything block you unexpectedly?”
11
Run & interpret data enrichment (LOB view)
LOB steps 1–3Enrichment
“For the Chicago Warehouse, you want third-party property data. Run the enrichment and tell me what came back.”
What we observe
  • Find and trigger the Run Verisk enrichment task; understand the pending → retrieved → mapped lifecycle.
  • Recognize enrichment (API) sources vs. document sources in View Sources.
  • Read the enrichment response rendered as a FIELD / VALUE grid.
  • Reaction to the runtime notification surfaced mid-lifecycle.
Success criteria
  • Runs enrichment and identifies at least one enriched attribute and its provider.
Post-task probe
  • “Which third-party sources would you actually want, and how would you weigh them against broker data?”
12
Wrap-up: is this submission ready?
Step 9Synthesis
“Taking everything together — would you consider this submission ready for the next stage of underwriting? Walk me through how you'd confirm that.”
What we observe
  • Whether they can self-assess completeness (no open Needs Attention, discrepancies resolved).
  • What they'd still want that the prototype doesn't yet show.
Success criteria
  • Gives a reasoned ready / not-ready judgment grounded in the prototype's signals.

8 · Debrief & SUS

Open debrief questions

  1. What was the most confusing moment in the whole flow?
  2. Where did the AI agent save you time — and where did it get in your way or feel risky?
  3. How does this compare to how you intake a submission today?
  4. What would you not want the agent to do automatically?
  5. If you could change one thing, what would it be?
  6. Would you trust this to prepare a submission for your review? Why / why not?

System Usability Scale (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree)

  1. I think that I would like to use this system frequently.
  2. I found the system unnecessarily complex.
  3. I thought the system was easy to use.
  4. I think I would need support of a technical person to use this system.
  5. I found the various functions in this system were well integrated.
  6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system.
  7. I would imagine most people would learn this system very quickly.
  8. I found the system very cumbersome to use.
  9. I felt very confident using the system.
  10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system.
